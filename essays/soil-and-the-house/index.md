## What do I actually want?

The first night after my breakup, I asked myself a simple question: what do I really want from a relationship? Not what I'm supposed to want, but what I would build if I were honest.

I found that a relationship has two levels for me. The first is the soil. The second is the house you build on top of it.

<!-- truncate -->

## Level 1: the soil

The soil is feeling. It's attraction, hormones, the spark when two people touch, kiss, and spend time together. We are not robots. We are mammals, so feelings are always there.

I rarely mention this level, not because it doesn't matter, but because it's the ground everything stands on. As an ENFP, I can't imagine a relationship without it. Without the soil, you're just two robots sharing a schedule.

The soil isn't the goal, though. For me it works like a hormone boost: someone can give me an instant spike of energy. But I also get that energy from sports, learning something new, and new experiences. The soil is necessary, but it isn't what I'm looking for.

## Level 2: the house

On top of the soil, people build a house. I call it the safety net: what a relationship gives you beyond feelings. It's more than "friends with benefits," which only covers the body. A safety net covers life: sharing energy, walking toward the same goal, and protecting each other from a chaotic world.

Everyone builds a different house:

- **Emotional support**: someone to lean on when life is hard.
- **Economic protection**: a second income, shared costs, a cushion.
- **Companionship**: a partner to face an uncertain world with.

I don't need most of these right now. My emotional support comes from myself and from feminism, and it's strong enough to carry me through life. Money matters to me, but I want to earn it myself. I'm smart enough to, and my lifestyle is minimalist anyway. A partner would probably make me spend more, not less.

## My safety net: freedom to move

What I actually want from the house is diplomatic. After years in Europe, in Italy and in Spain, I know where I want to end up: a small town with beautiful trees, a beach, or mountains. Maybe northern Europe. I'd even learn to drive for it.

Right now I can't travel freely. I need visas. I want a better passport, the freedom to move and settle where I choose. That's the safety net I'd look for.

I also have a plan B. I can reach all of this on my own. It just takes longer. A partner would be a faster bus, not the only road. That means I don't need a relationship. I can choose one.

## No need is nobler than another

At first I felt a little proud that I didn't need emotional or economic support. Then I realized my need is big too. A passport and a new home country is no small thing to ask of a relationship. So I can't judge anyone who builds their house out of emotional support. We all bring needs to the table.

What matters is knowing which house you want, saying it out loud, and finding someone who is happy to build it with you, with the soil still alive underneath.

I'm almost thirty, two nights after a breakup, and I finally know what I'm building. I'm not in a rush. I'm just clear.
